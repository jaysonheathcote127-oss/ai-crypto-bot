## Rules from the updated FX plan (Jack's methodology, not independently validated)

### Analysis and conditions

1. Start each session by classifying conditions. External highs/lows breaking and new structure forming = trending. No external breaks, price oscillating between levels = ranging. The model must match conditions.
2. Weekly/daily/4H provide direction and key levels. 1H/30m/15m identify structure and confirm breaks. 3m/5m/15m build the executable setup.
3. Identify weak structure as the destination before seeking an entry. It is associated with a leg failing to make a new high/low before rejecting. External weak structure carries more weight; internal weak levels can matter within ranges.
4. Protected structure is the origin of the move that broke structure. Return to this area provides continuation entries. Its failure warns of a direction change; wait for confirmation of a new direction.
5. A structure-breaking candle needs at least half its body beyond the level, strong directional delivery and little adverse wick. Cycle one timeframe below and above; e.g. 3m/5m/15m. Prefer external breaks. Avoid chasing after 2–3 same-direction breaks without a major pullback.
6. Check DXY. Opposing DXY movement toward its weak structure/gap is preferred confluence, not an absolute requirement. No claim that this relation generalises unchanged to all crypto assets.

### Entry models

- Trending: protected high/low retest plus sweep and failed close beyond the level, or divergence plus rejection. Internal protected variants require recent BOS and pro-trend context. Aim toward weak structure.
- Ranging: formed lower-timeframe range with 3+ touch points across top/bottom. Prefer NC+S (sweep the edge, fail to close outside, then rejection). Plain NC and NC+DIV variants also described.
- Double confirmation: overlapping NC/NC+S models on two timeframes near the same level, with a weak structural target. Check neighbouring timeframes.
- Divergence: minimum 3m/5m, stronger on higher timeframes; at protected structure for trending or two points near range edges for ranging. It still needs a rejection candle. Exact oscillator/settings were not specified in the plan rows read.
- Rejection must close before entry, with a large directional body and no more than 15–20% adverse wick. The narrative distinguishes the sweep/failed close from the next rejection candle; this requires careful sequencing in code.
- 9 EMA is minor entry confluence, the least weighted factor.
- Risk entry exception: range entry against weak-structure direction, reduced size, closer opposing 3m/5m structure target, more aggressive breakeven. This is an explicit exception to the general instruction always to trade toward weak structure.

### Stops, targets and management

- Set stop and risk before entry; stop must cover relevant protected structure/sweep wick. Plan suggests approximately 2 pips extra where appropriate. FX pip buffers cannot be copied as universal crypto percentages.
- Target nearest opposing weak structure. Minimum 1.3R must be available; skip if it is not. Do not reposition a stop merely to force acceptable R.
- Normally move stop to cost-adjusted breakeven after first internal structure break; earlier for risk entries approaching the break with momentum. Failed breaks or opposing NC can also trigger protection; consolidation introduces discretion.
- Plan describes cost coverage as 1–2 pips beyond entry; actual fills and costs still require measurement.
- Default hold to structural target. If extending target, trail under higher lows / above lower highs.
- **Unresolved contradiction:** Targets and Exit Criteria sections specify a 5m close across 9 EMA against the position for trailing exits; EMAs section specifies 15m. Do not silently choose one.
- Updated plan account-based risk: 1% challenge phase; 0.5–0.75% live/funded live, reduced for risk entries.
- **Unresolved contradiction:** course Week 13 says A+ up to 2%, A 1%, B 0.5%, C zero. This differs from the updated plan's account-based risk caps; version/context needs resolution before implementation.

### Sharper definitions and additional conflicts

1. Week 7 specifies a directional interaction sequence: sweep/NC at level, take an internal 5m low, print a lower high, then break structure; invert for bullish direction. This is a directional read, not by itself an entry. For internal continuation after a trend shift, wait for the second internal break; the first only establishes the shift. After 2–3 breaks without a meaningful retracement, it describes waiting for a 50% pullback of the HTF move. Precise causal swing identification still requires operational definition.
2. Week 8 distinguishes a meaningful sweep from a mere tick; after a tick, wait for a second sweep. No numerical tick-versus-sweep distance is provided. Its liquidity explanations are the course's theory, not independently demonstrated causal evidence.
3. Week 10 introduces a 50% threshold in its NC definition, while NC+S descriptions elsewhere say no close beyond the level. The reference denominator and whether this is body-position or closing-price tolerance need clarification. Do not reduce every NC to a simple close-back-inside rule and claim fidelity.
4. Week 11 identifies **RSI** divergence: long continuation example price higher low / RSI lower low; short example price lower high / RSI higher high. This resolves the initial oscillator-identity gap, but exact RSI length, divergence pivot settings and repaint/confirmation behaviour remain unknown.
5. Risk entry is explicitly defined in Week 11 and August 24 Discord as **no confirmed MTF/HTF weak target**, not simply any countertrend trade. Week 11 describes an MTF break and protected entry; the updated plan describes a ranging counter-target exception. Treat this as unresolved scope/version variation.
6. Week 13: A retains model and structural target but lacks DXY alignment; B is reduced-risk entry; C is model alone without support and is skipped. Skip plain NC without sweep or alignment, poor-sweep risk entries, and ranges too tight. Week 11 also says ranges can be too wide. No measurable range-width limits supplied.
7. Minimum payoff conflict: updated plan requires **1.3R**; weeks 11 and 14 describe **1R**. Historical recaps include 1R targets. Do not pool these as one unchanged rule set.
8. Timeframe conflict: updated plan/Week 11 mostly require at least 3m/5m divergence; Week 12 says 1m/3m/5m, and recaps regularly use 1m entries. These are real refinements/variants, not one consistent minimum timeframe.
9. Week 14 gives a possible partial explanation for EMA variation: 5m trailing exit with volume, potentially continue trailing on 15m after aligned 15m momentum. It does not fully resolve the updated plan's contradictory 5m/15m exit wording.
10. Week 14 calls three windows with one trade each a ceiling of roughly ten weekly trades. Three windows over five weekdays would instead permit fifteen; actual availability/attendance may explain fewer trades, but the arithmetic wording is inconsistent.
